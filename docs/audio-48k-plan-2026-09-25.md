# Native 48 kHz audio: the platform's share (2026-09-25)

Quake plays at 48 kHz because that is what it asks for, and the hard rule is
that an application gets the rate it asks for. Most of what 48 kHz costs it
is its own: it resamples every sound effect to the output rate on load (a
~8.5 MB sound cache at 48 kHz, which is what forces the 20 MB hunk and the
paging) and mixes 4.4x the samples of 11 kHz. Neither is ours. This plan is
about the part that is: SDL2's ALSA backend -> our `s31route` plugin
(rootfs/s31route.c) -> `plug` -> `hw` -> the I2S driver
(linux-71-port/sound/soc/espressif/esp32s31-i2s.c) -> AHB GDMA cyclic DMA
(drivers/dma/esp32s31-ahb-gdma.c) -> ES8389.

Two items, 4 and 5 of the 2026-09-24 ideas list, plus the measurement that
decides whether either is worth its build. Standing rules apply: apps
stock, no rate forced, fresh boot per arm, five runs where fps is the
judge, and **audio is verified by ear** - instruments have called this path
working while it played noise (memory: s31-audio-verify-by-ear).

## What the code does today

- **s31route is an ioplug with `mmap_rw = 0`** (s31route.c:634). SDL2
  writes into the plugin's ring; `route_transfer()` (:423) forwards each
  period with `snd_pcm_writei()` (:470) into the slave, which is `plug:` over
  `hw:` for the codec sink. So a period is copied at least twice in
  userspace (SDL2 -> ioplug ring -> plug's buffer, or -> the kernel via
  writei's copy_from_user) and every transfer costs the plug layer's
  SYNC_PTR ioctls. The plugin's own comment records how expensive this layer
  was before its ring sizing was fixed (prboom 15 vs 32 fps).
- **The I2S hardware caps a period at 4,092 bytes** (esp32s31-i2s.c:580):
  1,023 frames of S16 stereo, 21.3 ms at 48 kHz, so at least 47 period
  interrupts and 47 audio-thread wakeups a second, against 11.6 at 11 kHz.
  The cap is the GDMA descriptor's 12-bit length.
- **The PCM pointer is period-granular.** `esp32s31_ahb_tx_status()`
  (ahb-gdma.c:866) reports residue as `len - tx_node * period`: the hardware
  position only moves when a descriptor completes. ALSA's avail, delay and
  SDL2's wait all see a staircase.
- **An audio thread was seen sleeping in `soc_pcm_pointer`** (wchan of the
  SDL audio thread during play, 2026-09-24). A pointer callback should never
  be a place a task sleeps; the GDMA side only takes a spinlock, so the
  sleep is somewhere between the ALSA core and our DAI/component callbacks.

## Step 0. Measure the audio path's share (no build, 30 min)

1. Fresh boot, X11 TyrQuake at its native 48 kHz, `-mem 20`, fullscreen
   timedemo; pin the whole game to CPU0 (`/root/pin -p 0 <pid>`) so the
   hart sampler sees the audio thread, capture 8 s of samples and the maps,
   resolve with `h1s-report.py --symdir` including symbol tables for
   libasound, libSDL2, `libasound_module_pcm_s31route.so`. Sum by file and by
   kernel category (sound/, dma/, the ALSA ioctls).
2. Per-thread rates over 20 s: the SDL audio thread's voluntary and
   involuntary switches (`/proc/<pid>/task/<tid>/status`), the i2s DMA IRQ/s
   from `/proc/interrupts`, and `hw_params` / `status` from
   `/proc/asound/card0/pcm0p/sub0/` (rate, format, period and buffer size -
   **confirm S16_LE stereo 48000 end to end, no rate plugin anywhere**).
3. Sample `/proc/<tid>/stack` of the audio thread 50 times over 10 s; the
   frames above `soc_pcm_pointer` say where it sleeps.

**Decision rule.** If plugin + libasound + kernel sound/DMA is under 2% of
CPU0 pinned, and the audio thread is not sleeping inside the pointer path,
record the numbers and stop: there is nothing here worth a build. Any
resampling or format conversion found in step 0.2 is fixed first, before
items 4 and 5, because it would dwarf both.

## Item 4. A zero-copy routing plugin

**Aim.** One copy per period, from the application's ring straight into the
DMA buffer, and no `plug` layer on the codec path.

**Change (rootfs/s31route.c, ours).** For the codec sink, open the slave as
`hw:` with `SND_PCM_ACCESS_MMAP_INTERLEAVED` instead of `plug:`, when the
application's format, rate and channels are ones the hardware accepts
directly (the common case: S16_LE stereo 44.1/48 kHz); keep today's `plug:`
path as the fallback for anything else and for the A2DP loopback sink. In
`route_transfer()` replace `snd_pcm_writei()` with `snd_pcm_mmap_begin()` /
copy the ioplug areas into the returned hw areas / `snd_pcm_mmap_commit()`.
That removes plug's intermediate buffer and its per-transfer ioctls. Full
zero-copy (the application writing into the DMA ring itself) is not
available through ioplug; one memcpy of 192 kB/s is the floor, and it is
cheap next to what it replaces.

**Hazard, checked before anything else.** The DMA buffer is allocated
cached on this SoC (memory: s31-dma-cache-coherency). When userspace writes
it through an mmap, nothing in the kernel sees the write, so the driver must
either map it uncached or write back the period before the DMA reads it.
Prove it with a test tone through the new path: `speaker-test -c2 -t sine
-f 440 -r 48000` and a sweep, **by ear**, and a captured loopback if the
codec's internal DAC->ADC path can be routed (s31-audio-noise-investigation
has the register notes). Noise, crackle or a wrong pitch kills the arm
until the driver is fixed.

**Arms.** One boot, `S31ROUTE_DIRECT=0/1` env switch read by the plugin at
open: step 0's sample shares and switch rates for each, then five fresh-boot
48 kHz `-mem 20` timedemos per arm.

**Kill rule.** Plugin + libasound + sound kernel share not down by at least
1 point of CPU0, or any audible artefact, or any xrun in 5 minutes of play
that the old path does not have (xruns: `S31ROUTE_TRACE=1` logs them).

## Item 5. The pointer path and the period cap

**5a. Why a task sleeps in `soc_pcm_pointer`.** Read the stacks from step
0.3. Likely candidates to check against them: a mutex or `snd_soc_dpcm`
lock taken on the pointer path by our DAI or component callbacks, a
`clk_get_rate` or regmap read inside a callback that is supposed to be
atomic, or an `i2c` codec access reached from pointer/delay. Fix is in our
driver (esp32s31-i2s.c) or the machine glue; mainline ASoC and the ES8389
driver are not patched (only our port's glue is).

**5b. Sub-period position.** Make `esp32s31_ahb_tx_status()` report the
live position inside the current descriptor (read the channel's current
descriptor address / remaining-length register if the AHB GDMA exposes it -
confirm against /opt/esp-idf's soc/esp32s31 ahb_dma_struct.h in the
container) instead of whole periods. ALSA then reports real avail and
delay, SDL2 can wake later and write more per wake, and underrun margins are
exact rather than a period pessimistic.

**5c. Larger periods.** Lift the 4,092-byte period cap by letting one period
span several chained descriptors in the cyclic setup (the descriptor length
limit stays; the period interrupt fires on the last descriptor of each
period). At 48 kHz a 4,096-frame period (85 ms, two periods = 170 ms of
latency, which Quake does not notice) cuts period interrupts and audio
thread wakeups from ~47/s to ~12/s. The application still chooses its
period; this only lets a larger one exist.

**Arms.** One kernel build carrying 5a-5c, each behind a module parameter
(default off) so the A/B is one boot: step 0's measurements per arm, the
i2s IRQ/s, the audio thread's switch rate, then five fresh-boot 48 kHz
timedemos with the winning combination. Audio by ear after every change;
the capture path shares this DMA engine (patches/0010 cyclic RX residue),
so a microphone recording must also be checked.

**Kill rule.** 5a ships if it removes the sleep, whatever the fps. 5b and
5c ship only if the audio thread's wakeups fall by half with no new xruns
and the combined share falls by at least 1 point of CPU0.

## Order and effort

| step | build | board time | decides |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 measure | none | 30 min | whether 4 and 5 are worth building |
| 5a pointer sleep | kernel | 1 h | a correctness bug if confirmed |
| 4 direct plugin | userspace only | 2 h | the userspace copies and ioctls |
| 5b, 5c position and periods | kernel | 2 h | wakeups and interrupts |

Queued behind the running board work (tracks 3-4 and the paging plan). The
by-ear checks need the user at the board; everything else runs unattended,
and any arm that needs an ear is left enabled only on the test copy until it
has been heard.
