4. ZB_fill16 - THE CLEARS (report only)
  Two calls a frame, 43,216 instructions each: QuakeSpasm's one glClear
  of depth and colour (glopClear -> zep_clear -> ZB_fill16, and
  zdb_clear_colour -> ZB_fill16), 153,600 B each at 320x240.
  - Depth: the epoch trick is tried every frame and never fits. zep_clear
    needs the last epoch's range plus an eighth above zmax + 2 to stay
    under 2^16; Quake stores near depths (the view model at glDepthRange
    0..0.3 is s >= 45,875 of 65,535), so the test at zep_clear+0x82 takes
    the real clear on every counted frame. No 16-bit epoch scheme can
    avoid it without giving up depth bits (the ping-pong the file rejects).
  - Colour: the game asks for it (gl_clear), and a fused "skip the clear
    where the frame will overwrite" needs to know that before the frame
    is drawn; steering the game's config is off the table.
  - Why it costs 2.8%: 193 of its 227 board samples are two store
    instructions of the 16-store loop (sw +0x8: 100, sw +0x18: 93) - the
    loop runs at PSRAM write-allocate speed: ~300 kB in ~3.2 ms of a
    ~115 ms frame, ~95 MB/s. It is already 18 instructions a 64-byte line;
    only fewer bytes or no CPU would help.
  - A PPA fill instead: NOT worth the driver work. Ceiling 2.8% of CPU0
    (~+0.25 fps at 8.7), and it gives most of that back: (1) the colour
    and depth buffers are user memory (libGL's heap, or the colour buffer
    in the client's shared-memory segment), virtually contiguous only -
    the PPA/GDMA would need a descriptor per 4 kB page or the buffers
    moved into driver-allocated CMA and mapped into the client, a new
    interface in our driver and in the library's buffer management;
    (2) the D-cache must be written back / invalidated over the 300 kB
    before the DMA and nothing may be dirty in it afterwards (the cache
    maintenance that measured expensive here, s31-cache-maint-stays-in-
    flash); (3) the world draw starts right after glClear, so the CPU
    waits for the fill unless it is issued at the previous swap, and the
    DMA competes for the same PSRAM bandwidth the first spans need; (4)
    the render still misses on every line afterwards, as now. Realistic
    gain ~1% against a kernel + library change; the memory note "offload
    is not purchasable below ~128 kB" sits right at 150 kB. If the clears
    ever need to go, the cheaper lever is a lazy per-row clear (a row
    cleared by the first span that touches it, the rest at swap), which
    merges the clear's line fetch with the render's - a filler-wide
    change, not attempted.

5. NOT DONE, AND WHY
  - The rest of ZB_fillTriangleGeneralMT: ~200 instructions of set-up a
    triangle (the planes, ztri_setup's edges) and the 64-a-row loop of
    the non-world MT batches (1,013 rows a frame): 0.38 M a frame left,
    flat on the board. A plane set-up shared with the tri path would save
    tens a triangle; not in the time box.
  - The last 74 a span in the tri filler: the ten fmadds are the span
    values (kept exact, so not stepped per row); the rest are the batch's
    constants reloaded because the pixels hold every register. Stepping
    the span values per row would round differently: not bit-identical.
  - gl_vertex4f (0.9 M, 195 a vertex): straight-line and flat on the
    board; see 1.
  - The other one-unit fillers (zf1_blend 0.91 M, zf8_sbar 0.77 M,
    ZB_fillTriangleGeneral 0.74 M, zf8_alias_bl 0.60 M) still pay a call
    a span; the same tri-level treatment applies to each, one at a time.
